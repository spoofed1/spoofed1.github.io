A little help, if you need it.

How do I turn on Developer Mode?

On your iPhone, open Settings → Privacy & Security → Developer Mode. Turn it on and tap Restart. After restarting, unlock your iPhone and confirm Turn On. You must do this in Settings; Spoofed cannot enable it for you.

If Developer Mode is missing, complete computer pairing first and check again. If you use Xcode on a Mac, connect and unlock the iPhone, then select it in Xcode’s Device Hub. Contact support if the option still does not appear.

Why does Spoofed ask to add a VPN?

This permission creates a local connection to your iPhone’s developer services so Spoofed can simulate location. It is not an internet privacy VPN. Tap Allow when prompted; Spoofed handles the connection automatically. If you previously used LocalDevVPN, turn it off and disable its Connect On Demand setting before using Spoofed.

I’m opening this on my iPhone

The helper runs on your computer. Open spoofed1.github.io on your Mac or Windows PC to download it. You can use Share Setup Link in Spoofed to send the address to your computer.

My computer shows a security warning

This preview is not signed with an Apple Developer ID or a Windows publisher certificate. macOS or Windows may ask for approval or block it. Download only from this page’s GitHub release links. On Mac, Apple’s guide to opening apps safely explains the available options. On Windows, check that you downloaded the expected release before deciding whether to continue. Managed computers may require your administrator.

My iPhone isn’t showing up

Unlock your iPhone and use a cable that supports data. On Mac, check that it appears in Finder. On Windows, install or open Apple Devices and check that your phone appears there. Approve Trust, then return to the helper. If several phones are connected, choose yours in the helper.

The helper asks me to open Spoofed

Install the latest Spoofed iPhone app, open Setup, and tap Receive from Computer. Keep that screen open and the iPhone unlocked. Receiving expires after ten minutes; tap it again if needed. Stop any active simulation before replacing pairing.

Does anything get uploaded?

The helper does not upload your pairing, device identifiers, or location. It uses the computer’s local Apple USB service and transfers pairing into Spoofed. The computer keeps its normal Apple trust record; the helper does not save a separate copy of your remote pairing. GitHub hosts this page and the downloads.

Do I need the helper every time?

Usually just for initial pairing. You can close the helper afterward. If you reset device trust or pairing stops working after an iOS change, run setup again. Keep Developer Mode enabled. Spoofed includes the local connection needed for location simulation.

My location is not changing

Unlock your iPhone, check that Developer Mode is on, and follow any instructions shown in Spoofed. In Settings → Privacy & Security → Local Network, allow Spoofed access. Try connecting to Wi-Fi if setup cannot connect. On cellular, follow the app’s recovery steps if offered; cellular startup may not work on every device or iOS version.

My old location still appears after Stop

Other apps may take a little time to show your real location again. Keep Location Services enabled and wait for a fresh location. If Spoofed says Stop was not confirmed, follow its retry instructions. If Stop keeps failing, restart your iPhone.

What has been tested?

This is an early preview. Builds and automated checks do not replace testing with a physical iPhone. The helper distinguishes delivery from confirmation by Spoofed; your first developer connection verifies the stored pairing works for location simulation.
